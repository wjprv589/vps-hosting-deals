## Three rules buried in the fine print (read before ordering)

None of these affect everyone, but all of them live in plan descriptions and panel behavior rather than sales pages, and getting caught costs real money or real downtime.

**Migration can shrink your transfer quota.** Moving a CN2-series plan into a regular KVM datacenter cuts its monthly transfer allocation to one third. A 1 TB allowance becomes 333 GB — fatal for a traffic-serving site. Decide before you click, since the panel makes migration a one-step operation.

**Hong Kong plans cannot migrate.** The Basic and GIA-E lines move freely between datacenters; Hong Kong CN2 GIA does not. It's the physically closest, lowest-latency option (under 50ms), which is exactly why it's fixed in place. Only buy it if the location fits your use case permanently.

**Not every location carries the same SLA.** The site advertises a 99.9% uptime guarantee on its overview page, while the shopping cart pages list 99.95% for current plans — and a 99.99% SLA exists only for the USCA_5 Los Angeles location on the dedicated SLA series, which starts at $65.89/quarter. If uptime is contractual for you, match the location to the number.

The provider explicitly credits self-management for its low prices, and support won't configure your environment. Fine as a learning platform; wrong as a managed hosting substitute.
- **Audiences in Europe and the Americas.** The GIA premium is spent entirely on China-bound routing. Western traffic rides ordinary international transit like any other US VPS. In that scenario, the cheaper competitors above are the rational choice.
- **Anyone needing serious DDoS mitigation.** Nothing in the provider's published materials addresses high-volume attack protection. That requirement points to specialized providers.
